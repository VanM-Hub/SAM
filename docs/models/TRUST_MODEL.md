# Trust Establishment

Trust is established when a governed decision demonstrably complies with constitutional principles.

It is not established by assertion.

It is established through evidence that a decision was correctly governed.

---

# Trust Maintenance

Trust is maintained by keeping governed conduct consistent over time.

Consistency across independent sources increases trust.

Continuity of correct governance preserves trust.

---

# Trust Verification

Trust is verified by checking that evidence independently reflects governed conduct.

Verification strengthens confidence.

Reproducible and independently confirmed evidence is stronger than repeated assertions.

---

# Trust Degradation

Trust naturally degrades over time.

Causes include software updates, configuration changes, provider evolution, environment changes, and obsolete documentation.

Conflict should never be ignored: identify conflict, explain disagreement, reduce confidence, and request additional evidence.

---

# Trust Recovery

Trust can be recovered when degradation is corrected.

Recovery requires re-establishing demonstrable compliance with constitutional principles.

Where material for Trust Recovery does not yet exist in this document, it is reported as a documentation gap and is not invented here.

# Operational Mechanisms

The following are operational mechanisms that help establish, verify, and preserve Trust.

They are not definitions of Trust.

## Trust Dimensions (Operational Mechanism)

- **Source Reliability** – Official documentation > Community > Opinion
- **Verification** – Independently verifiable evidence is more trusted
- **Consistency** – Agreement across independent sources increases trust
- **Recency** – Newer evidence is generally preferred
- **Reproducibility** – Evidence that can be reproduced is stronger
- **Completeness** – Incomplete evidence reduces trust
- **Independence** – Independent confirmation > repetition of same source

## Trust Levels (Operational Mechanism)

- **Very High** – Authoritative, verified, recent, reproducible, consistent
- **High** – Reliable with minor limitations
- **Moderate** – Useful but incomplete or partially verified
- **Low** – Significant uncertainty
- **Very Low** – Weak, conflicting, outdated
- **Unknown** – Trust cannot yet be established

## Evidence Reliability (Operational Mechanism)

Evidence answers "What information exists?"

Reliability of evidence supports, but does not define, Trust.

## Thinking Protocol (Operational Mechanism)

The Thinking Protocol positions Trust evaluation within a broader reasoning flow:

Observe → Understand → Collect Evidence → **Evaluate Trust** → Assess Risk → Decision

The Thinking Protocol is one mechanism that helps build and verify Trust.

It is not a source of the definition of Trust.

## Conflicting Evidence (Operational Mechanism)

When evidence conflicts, confidence is reduced and additional evidence is requested.

---

# Trust Boundary

Trust is distinct from:

- **Security** – Security protects assets; it contributes to Trust but is not identical to Trust.
- **Reliability** – Reliability reflects consistent operation; it supports Trust but is not Trust.
- **Compliance** – Compliance verifies conformance; it contributes to Trust but is not identical to it.
- **Auditability** – Auditability enables verification; it serves Trust but is not Trust itself.

These four may contribute to Trust.

They are not, by themselves, definitions of Trust.
