# Purpose



The Trust Model defines how the SAM Framework evaluates the reliability of available evidence.



Trust is not certainty. Trust represents confidence that evidence accurately reflects reality.



---



# Philosophy



Evidence answers "What information exists?" Trust answers "How reliable is that information?"



---



# Position within the Thinking Protocol



Observe → Understand → Collect Evidence → **Evaluate Trust** → Assess Risk → Decision



---



# Trust Dimensions



\- **Source Reliability** – Official documentation > Community > Opinion

\- **Verification** – Independently verifiable evidence is more trusted

\- **Consistency** – Agreement across independent sources increases trust

\- **Recency** – Newer evidence is generally preferred

\- **Reproducibility** – Evidence that can be reproduced is stronger

\- **Completeness** – Incomplete evidence reduces trust

\- **Independence** – Independent confirmation > repetition of same source



---



# Trust Levels



\- **Very High** – Authoritative, verified, recent, reproducible, consistent

\- **High** – Reliable with minor limitations

\- **Moderate** – Useful but incomplete or partially verified

\- **Low** – Significant uncertainty

\- **Very Low** – Weak, conflicting, outdated

\- **Unknown** – Trust cannot yet be established



---



# Conflicting Evidence



Conflict should never be ignored. Instead: identify conflict, explain disagreement, reduce confidence, request additional evidence.



---



# Trust Decay



Trust naturally decreases over time due to software updates, configuration changes, provider evolution, environment changes, obsolete documentation.



---



# Relationship with Decision \& Risk Models



Trust provides evidence quality assessment. Decision determines if trust is sufficient. Risk evaluates consequences independently.



---



# Summary



Trust Model evaluates evidence reliability across multiple dimensions, enabling transparent and evidence-based operational reasoning.
